Bank home loans come in two broad types. Fixed-rate packages hold the interest rate for a set period, often the first few years, giving predictable instalments. Floating-rate packages are pegged to a benchmark — usually SORA — plus a spread, so the instalment changes when the benchmark moves. When a fixed period ends, the rate typically reverts to a floating rate.

The HDB concessionary loan is a third kind. Its rate, currently {policy:rate.hdb.concessionary}, is reviewed quarterly in line with the CPF Ordinary Account rate, so it can change, but historically it has moved very little.

Worked example: e.g. on a `S$400,000` loan over `20` years, a rate move from `3.2%` to `4.2%` lifts the instalment from about `S$2,260` to about `S$2,470`. A floating-rate borrower feels this at the next reset; a fixed-rate borrower only when the fixed period ends.

Tip: Many bank packages have a lock-in period with a penalty for early repayment or refinancing, so those terms are part of comparing loans, not just the headline rate.

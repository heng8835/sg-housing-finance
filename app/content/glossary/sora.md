SORA is the volume-weighted average rate at which banks borrow Singapore dollars from each other overnight, without collateral. MAS calculates and publishes it every business day. It replaced older benchmarks such as SIBOR and SOR for Singapore-dollar loans.

Floating-rate home loans usually quote a compounded SORA average over a past period — such as one or three months — plus a fixed spread set by the bank. When SORA moves, the loan rate and therefore the instalment move with it at the next reset.

Worked example: e.g. if compounded SORA is `2.0%` and the spread is `0.8%`, the loan rate is `2.8%`; if SORA later rises to `3.0%`, the rate becomes `3.8%`.

Tip: SORA can move a lot within a few years. The HDB concessionary loan rate is not linked to SORA; it follows the CPF Ordinary Account rate.

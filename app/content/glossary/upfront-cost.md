Affordability has two sides: can the household keep up with the monthly instalment, and can it fund the purchase in the first place? Upfront cost answers the second question.

It typically includes the downpayment, any Cash Over Valuation, Buyer's Stamp Duty, Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty if it applies, the option and exercise fees (which count towards the price), legal and conveyancing fees, and valuation fees. Renovation and furnishing usually follow soon after.

Some items can be paid from CPF Ordinary Account savings; others must be cash — COV always, and with a bank loan at least {policy:downpayment.bank.cash_min} of the price. Housing grants credited to CPF reduce the total.

Worked example: e.g. a `S$600,000` flat bought with `S$15,000` COV: the cash needed includes that `S$15,000` plus any cash part of the downpayment, while stamp duty and legal fees might come from CPF.

Tip: The split between cash and CPF matters as much as the total, because cash is often the tighter constraint.

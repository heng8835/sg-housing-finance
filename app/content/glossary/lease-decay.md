Because an HDB flat returns to HDB at the end of its lease, its value must eventually fall towards zero. Lease decay describes that path. While a lot of lease remains, prices are driven mostly by location, size and the wider market; the effect of a short lease becomes more visible as fewer decades remain.

Financing rules can amplify the effect. Buyers of short-lease flats may face pro-rated CPF use and HDB loans if the lease does not cover them to age {policy:cpf.lease.cover_to_age}, which narrows the pool of buyers who can afford those flats.

Worked example: e.g. two similar four-room flats in the same town, one with `90` years left and one with `55` years left, may trade at quite different prices per square foot despite being the same size.

Tip: Lease decay in past transactions is an observed pattern, not a rule; future prices also depend on policy and demand.

The RPI summarises how HDB resale prices as a whole have moved over time. It is set relative to a base quarter, so a reading above the base value means prices are higher on average than in that quarter. HDB publishes a flash estimate as each quarter ends and the final figure a few weeks later.

The index accounts for differences in the mix of flats sold — such as type, location and age — so it reflects price trends better than a simple average of transaction prices.

Worked example: e.g. if the RPI rises from `180` to `189`, resale prices have risen by about `5%` on average over that period, though individual towns and flat types can move differently.

Tip: The RPI describes the whole market; this app's recent-sales benchmark looks at a specific block or town, which can diverge from the overall trend.

An amortisation schedule lists every instalment over the life of the loan and splits each one into interest and principal. Interest is charged on the balance still owed, so in the early years most of the instalment is interest. As the balance falls, the interest portion shrinks and more of each payment reduces the debt.

This is why the outstanding loan falls slowly at first and faster towards the end. It also explains why a partial prepayment made early in the loan saves more interest than the same amount paid late.

Worked example: e.g. on a `S$400,000` loan at an illustrative `3.2%`, the first month's interest is about `S$1,070` (`S$400,000 × 3.2% ÷ 12`). With an instalment of about `S$2,260`, only about `S$1,190` goes to principal that month.

Tip: The balance left after some years matters when thinking about selling or refinancing, because it has to be repaid from the sale proceeds or the new loan.

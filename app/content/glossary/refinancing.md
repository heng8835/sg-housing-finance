Refinancing means taking a new loan to pay off the old one. Borrowers do this when another lender offers a better rate or more suitable terms. Switching to a new package with the same bank is often called repricing.

For HDB flats, a borrower can move from an HDB loan to a bank loan, but cannot move from a bank loan back to an HDB loan. That makes leaving an HDB loan a one-way step.

Costs to weigh include legal and valuation fees (sometimes subsidised by the new lender) and any penalty if the current loan is still within its lock-in period. The new loan is assessed under MAS rules, which include some relaxations for owner-occupiers refinancing their own home.

Worked example: e.g. a `S$300,000` balance with `20` years left, moving from `4.2%` to `3.2%`, cuts the instalment from about `S$1,850` to about `S$1,690` a month — a saving to compare against the switching costs.

Tip: Refinancing does not change the outstanding balance; it changes the rate and terms applied to it.

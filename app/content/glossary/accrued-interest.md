term: Accrued interest (CPF refund on sale)
short: The interest that CPF savings used for a flat would have earned in the Ordinary Account; it is refunded to CPF, with the principal, when the flat is sold.
formula: "Refund on sale = CPF principal used + accrued interest at the OA rate (compounded)"
policy_keys: [proposed.cpf.oa.rate]
related: [cpf-oa, cpf-housing, total-interest, psf]
level: intermediate
source: https://www.cpf.gov.sg/service/article/can-i-voluntarily-refund-a-portion-of-the-cpf-savings-i-used-for-my-property
---
When CPF savings go into a home, they stop earning interest in the Ordinary Account. To protect retirement savings, CPF tracks the interest those savings would have earned — at the OA rate of at least {policy:proposed.cpf.oa.rate} a year, compounded — as accrued interest.

When the flat is sold, the proceeds first repay any outstanding loan, then refund the CPF principal used plus accrued interest into the owners' CPF accounts. The money remains the owners' own savings, but it goes back into CPF rather than to them as cash.

Worked example: e.g. `S$100,000` of CPF used for a flat and left for `10` years at an illustrative `3%`, compounded yearly for simplicity, grows to about `S$134,000` to be refunded on sale.

Tip: If an HDB flat is sold at market value and the proceeds fall short of the full refund, owners generally do not have to top up the difference in cash. Owners can also make voluntary refunds to reduce the amount that builds up.

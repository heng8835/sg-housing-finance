The comparison table's future-value section looks at eight things that have historically been linked with how a resale flat's value holds up: how much lease will be left when you might sell, how the town's prices moved against HDB's Resale Price Index, whether the flat is priced below or above similar-lease sales, new MRT stations and major projects nearby, how much fresh supply (new BTO flats and flats reaching their MOP) is coming, the rental yield, how often flats in the block change hands, and how rare the flat type is in the town.

Each driver gets a score from `1` to `5` from fixed cut-offs, with a one-line reason. There is deliberately no overall score: the drivers pull in different directions, and how much each matters depends on your plans.

Worked example: e.g. a flat may score well on rental yield and liquidity but poorly on lease runway — a reminder to weigh the lease more if you plan to sell in ten years.

Tip: None of these drivers predicts prices. Master Plan zoning is not a committed project, rents are declared rents, and past momentum is not a guide to future returns.
